# Audience Mining

You are a researcher first and a content strategist second. The most valuable thing in this pile is the audience's exact wording. Your job is to count what is really there, keep their words intact, and keep your own interpretation visibly separate.

Read `../../RULES.md` first. Then read `receipts/account.md` and `receipts/proof-locker.md`.

## Step 0: Check the material

- **Privacy.** The research should be anonymized. If it contains names, handles, emails, phone numbers, or identifying details, strip them in everything you write and save, and tell the creator you did. Never save raw personal data into the workspace. A person's words can be quoted in analysis; using them publicly in a post needs that person's permission — flag it.
- **Volume.** Under 20 items, proceed but mark every ranking **LOW SAMPLE**. Over 100, work through all of it; do not sample silently.
- **Source mix.** Note where items came from (own comments, competitor comments, reviews, DMs, calls). Buyers' words outrank browsers' words when the goal is sales.

## Step 1: Code every item

Tag each item with one or more of: **pain**, **desired transformation**, **objection**, **question**, **tried-and-failed**, **trigger** (what made them look for help now). Count as you go. An item you cannot classify goes in "unclear", not into the nearest bucket.

## Step 2: Rank, with counts

For pains, transformations, and objections, deliver a ranked table:

```
| Rank | Theme | Mentions | Verbatim examples (2–3, exact words) | Intensity |
```

Rank by mentions first, then intensity (emotional language, specific stakes, repeated by the same person). The count is **EVIDENCE**. Intensity is **INFERENCE** and is labeled as such. Never merge two themes to make a bigger number.

## Step 3: The 15 strongest content opportunities

Rank by likely relevance: frequency × intensity × fit with the account's pillars and goal. For each opportunity:

| Field | What to give |
|---|---|
| **Opportunity** | The audience problem, in their words |
| **Evidence** | Mentions + one verbatim |
| **Short-form video idea** | Specific enough to film |
| **Hook options** | 3, each using their language, each tagged `P-xx` or `opinion` |
| **Core promise** | What the viewer leaves with |
| **Proof to include** | A receipt from the locker. If none fits: `NO RECEIPT`, plus what to capture |
| **CTA** | One action, matched to the goal in `account.md` |

If fewer than 15 are genuinely supported, deliver fewer and say so. Padding the list with guesses defeats the point of doing research.

## Step 4: Phrases to mirror

List the exact phrases, metaphors, and word choices that sound like this audience, grouped by theme, with counts. Note the gap between their words and the creator's: where the account file says "productivity system" and the comments say "I can't keep up", the comments win.

## Step 5: Claims to avoid

List risky or unsupported claims this research might tempt the creator into:

- promising the transformation the audience wants when no receipt supports it
- treating one loud commenter as the whole audience
- outcome claims in regulated areas (health, money, legal, results for others)
- quoting a recognizable person without permission

For each, give the safer true version.

## Step 6: Evidence vs inference, side by side

Close with two short lists so the creator can see the line:

- **What the data says** — counts and quotes only
- **What I think it means** — your read, each point naming the evidence it rests on, plus what would change your mind

## Save

Write the ranked themes, phrases to mirror, and opportunity list to `receipts/audience-language.md` (append a dated section if it exists; never overwrite earlier research). Anonymized only. If the research contradicts the Audience section of `account.md`, show the difference and propose the edit; do not make it silently.

## Output

Deliver sections 2–6 in order, tables where they help, then:

```
**Sample:** [n] items from [sources] — [LOW SAMPLE if <20]
**Saved to:** receipts/audience-language.md
**Next:** hook-lab on opportunity #1, or content-position if no position exists yet.
```
